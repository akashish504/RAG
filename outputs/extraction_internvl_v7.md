DOCUMENT_SUMMARY: The IDH Gender Tool Guide is an excel-based resource designed to assist IDH staff in integrating a gender lens into their processes. It is intended for use in facilitating gender conversations and identifying useful actions for businesses. The tool includes a structured approach with steps for initial screening, gender recommendations, and data collection. It emphasizes the importance of women in the agricultural labor force and how incorporating a gender perspective can lead to increased revenue and business growth. The guide provides specific instructions and examples to help users effectively utilize the tool.

## Slide 5: WHY IS GENDER IMPORTANT?

## THE OPPORTUNITY

Women comprise, on average, 43% of the agricultural labor force in developing countries, ranging from 20% in Latin America to more than 60% in Sub-Saharan Africa^1.

Closing gender gaps in agriculture could generate significant gains for the agriculture sector, as well as broader socio-economic outcomes for households:

- **Yields:** A study of maize farmers in Kenya found that an increase in women's empowerment by 1% led to a 6.4% increase in maize yield due to improved uptake of farming technologies and services^2.

- **Food security:** Closing yield gaps between men and women could raise total agricultural output in developing countries by 2.5-4% to feed an additional 100-150 million people^3.

- **Broader socio-economic benefits:** Increasing women's share of household income through supporting their agricultural livelihoods can help drive greater household-level businesses compared to an equivalent increase for men. Increasing women's income by $10 achieves the same improvements in children's nutrition and health as an increase to a man's income of $110^4.

## THE CHALLENGE

Women and girls face limited access to key enablers that ensure an inclusive agriculture transformation

### Key enablers

- **Productivity drivers**
  - Inputs
  - Assets
  - Skills
  - Financial services
  - Time

- **Market linkages**
  - Market intelligence
  - Mobility
  - Buyer linkages

- **Enabling environment**
  - Business, policy and legal environment
  - Infrastructure

### Skills: Literacy rates for rural women can be as low as 30% in some African countries^5 which can hinder their use of more complex digital platforms

### Time: Globally, women spend an average of 3X on unpaid care work and domestic work than men, reducing the time they have available to do other activities, like attend a training^6

### Mobility: In many countries, women cannot make decisions about where to go, limiting their access to markets and services delivered outside of the home (e.g., in Nigeria, only 8% of married women can make personal decisions about visits to family)^7

---

^1 CGIAR, "Empowering Africa's women farmers," 2019; ^2 Gracious M. Diro, et al., "Women's empowerment in agriculture and agricultural productivity: Evidence from rural maize farmer households in western Kenya," 2018; ^3 FAO, "The State of Food and Agriculture," 2011; ^4 FAO, "FAO Committee on Food Security," 2011; ^5 Prepared by the SOFA Team and Cheryl Doss, "The Role of Women in Agriculture," 2011; ^6 IFC, "Investing in Women Along Agribusiness Value Chain," 2016; ^7 Dahlberg, "It's about time: The case for valuing women and girls' unpaid care work," 2019.

## Slide 7: WHAT GUIDELINES CAN YOU FOLLOW WHEN FACILITATING GENDER CONVERSATIONS?

1. Prepare a thoughtful business case and answers to difficult questions. Invest time identifying the business case for a potential partner in advance and practice responses to difficult questions.

2. Celebrate successes while keeping in mind improvement areas. Reinforce what partners are doing well and build connections between strengths and weaknesses to encourage momentum.

3. "Move at the speed of trust". Introduce sensitive topics (e.g., gender-based violence, salary levels) slowly, taking stock of the relationship to determine whether the partner feels secure enough to engage productively in dialogue.

4. Build on common ground. Frame objectives, questions, and suggestions using familiar examples (e.g., local-level) to establish linkages between abstract concepts and partners' lived experiences.

5. Suspend judgement. Instead of passing judgement, communicate with respect and open-mindedness, using negative responses as an opportunity for dialogue.

## Slide 8: WHAT THE GENDER TOOL WILL AND WILL NOT DO?

- The gender tool will:
- Provide prompts to facilitate a conversation with IDH partner(s); the conversation can be structured however makes the most sense (e.g., open-ended questions vs. asking questions verbatim), as long as the relevant information is collected
- Generate a long-list of ideas of potential actions an IDH partner(s) can take
- Collect data on key gender indicators to establish a starting point from which a partner can measure progress from
- The gender tool will not:
- Provide a script that will tell you verbatim what to say to the IDH partner
- Generate an exhaustive list of recommendations that is applicable to every project context or variation or that will ‘solve’ all gender challenges
- Cover all the support or information that IDH provides on gender (e.g., IDH Gender Toolkit, Farmfit Gender Expertise)

## Slide 19: EXPLANATION: RAPID GENDER ASSESSMENT

With the Rapid Gender Assessment, the IDH staff member will understand how far along IDH's current or potential partners are on their gender journey

### RAPID GENDER ASSESSMENT

Once you have answered the questions on Step 1, the partner or platform will fall into one of these three categories:

- **Gender unintentional**
  The IDH partner or platform does not take steps to understand or address the different needs and constraints of women and men in its internal processes, strategy, or service design (when applicable)

- **Gender intentional**
  The IDH partner or platform has taken steps to at least understand the different needs and constraints of women and men in its internal process, strategy, or service design (when applicable), with the goal of ensuring both women and men have access to resources

- **Gender transformative**
  The IDH partner or platform takes a data-driven approach to understand the different needs and constraints of women and men, and when applicable, tailoring services to ensure either that men and women have access to resources, control over the benefits of those resources or are working in an inclusive workplace

## Slide 39: Glossary of terms

- Glossary of terms
- The unpaid and often unrecognized work that is done both as a result of the climate crisis (e.g., greater time spent collecting water) and to adapt to or mitigate the climate crisis (e.g., investment in resilient farming practices)
- To be gender transformative means to address root causes of gender inequity to enhance women’s power and agency, for example by combatting entrenched social norms.
- To be gender intentional means to consider unique needs and experiences by gender and tailor products, services, interventions, or policies to lived realities.
- To be gender unintentional means to take an approach (e.g., to tailoring services) that does not consider differences between genders (e.g., a gender-blind approach), and can lead to reinforced or expanded inequality.
- A gender lens is an analytical approach which asks how women and men might experience or engage with a problem, product, service, intervention, or policy differently, and identifies how that may affect their comparative abilities to benefit from it.

### QUESTION: Gender strategy: Is gender equality a strategic goal for the potential partner organization that they communicate in documents (e.g., in strategic plan, internal or external targets, marketing documents)?

### Answer: If Yes:
- Excellent! If the potential IDH partner you are screening is interested in strengthening its strategic approach to gender equality, you may suggest adopting one or more of the following recommendations:
  - **HOW TO ADEQUATELY RESOURCE THE GENDER STRATEGY**
    - Determine staff perceptions on the value of the gender strategy (e.g., through an anonymous survey).
    - Clarify the amount of time and resources informally allocated to the gender strategy in order to develop an estimated budget for the gender strategy workload.
    - Reallocate staff time, tasks, and resources to support execution of the gender strategy, including by designating a technical staff member to drive the gender strategy and serve as the strategic focal point.
    - Identify measurable indicators that connect to priorities or outcomes established in the gender strategy such as the proportion of female employees that have been able to participate in company training, the ratio of income between female and male employees, etc.
    - Allocate resources for development of a measurement and evaluation framework, etc.
    - Establish reporting mechanisms to publicly track progress against gender-related KPIs.
    - HOW TO CODIFY THE STRATEGY INTERNALLY
      - Codify its approach to gender equality by outlining the strategy in a company document or memo processes for capturing, reporting, and disseminating this knowledge and learnings.

### Answer: If No or Unclear/Unknown
- To help the potential IDH partner begin considering gender equality a strategic goal, you may consider providing other IDH resources on the business case for gender equality (e.g., "Section 1: Context on the resources on the business case for gender equality" tool" in the accompanying tool, PowerPoint, IDH Gender Toolkit). In addition, you could suggest the partner can define how many male and female customers they aim to reach and/or how they want to support male and female employees, and create a plan that will help them achieve that target, recognizing that this may require a tailored approach for both men and women.

### QUESTION: Data collection: Does the potential partner collect data on staff or customers disaggregated by gender?

### Answer: If Yes:
- Excellent! If the potential IDH partner you are screening is interested in strengthening its gender-disaggregated data collection and analysis, you may suggest adopting one or more of the following recommendations:
  - **Collect data on staff recruitment and retention among women and men to understand where there might be opportunities to better support employees, reduce turnover, and ultimately save recruiting costs (e.g., data on data promotions, leadership breakdown, and training).
  - Collect data on the number of male and female customers, types of purchases made by each, and size of purchases to better understand consumption patterns and preferences that can help to better serve male and female customers.
  - Collect data on staff members' ability to reach this male and female customers (e.g., data on male and female workshop attendance) to identify those able to attract a diverse group and capture any lessons on why/how.

### Answer: If No or Unclear/Unknown
- To help the potential IDH partner begin collecting gender-disaggregated data, you may consider suggesting they:
  - Collect gender-disaggregated data on employee recruitment, pay, promotion, skills training, and turnover.
  - Collect gender-disaggregated data on male and female customers.

### QUESTION: Inclusive workplace: Does the potential partner have policies or practices to make the workplace inclusive for both women and men (e.g., equal payment policies, enforced sexual harassment policies, training on gender-based violence, hiring targets)?

### Answer: If Yes:
- Excellent! If the potential IDH partner you are screening is interested in strengthening its workplace inclusivity, you may suggest adopting one or more of the following recommendations:
  - **Develop and protect safe reporting procedures for victims of violence and/or harassment (e.g., trusted advisor - appointed members of staff survivors can confide in, emergency hotlines) for both employees and individuals engaged by employees (e.g., community members, smallholder farmers).
  - Regularly review and update disciplinary procedures, and conduct organization-wide training on violence or sexual harassment at the workplace and in external settings. When recruiting, make clear that both women and men have an equal opportunity to apply and be considered for a job (e.g., explicitly stating this in recruitment materials or conversations, featuring photos of both women and men in job advertisements, partnering with local education institutions to target both female and male students).
  - Assess the gap between current wages and a living wage, using the IDH Salary Matrix for living wage v.2.0.
  - Conduct a pay and promotion review/audit to investigate whether gender affects promotions; determine where men and women are doing 'equal work' and compare compensation in these areas using pay data.
  - Recruit and train both women and men to serve as leaders of marketing groups, outgrower schemes, and contract committees.

### Answer: If No or Unclear/Unknown
- To help the potential IDH partner begin having policies or practices to make the workplace inclusive, you may consider suggesting they:
  - Assess the current recruitment, retention, and advancement practices and diagnose where there might be challenges and install safeguards against violence and harassment.
